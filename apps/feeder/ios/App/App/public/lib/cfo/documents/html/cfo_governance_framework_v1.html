1. Purpose Of This Framework

This Governance Framework defines how the Chief Financial Officer (CFO) establishes, operates, and maintains the financial governance environment for Crave'n. It provides the enterprise-level guardrails that ensure discipline, accountability, transparency, and alignment across all financial and operational domains.

This framework is designed to mirror the standards used in Fortune 500 technology and logistics organizations, adapted to Crave'n's high-growth environment.

2. Governance Scope

The CFO's governance authority applies to the following domains:

  • Financial strategy, planning, and modeling
  • Cash management and treasury operations
  • Financial reporting and business performance analytics
  • Accounting, control activities, and financial integrity
  • Compliance with all financial, regulatory, and tax obligations
  • Investor readiness and capital formation processes
  • Cross-functional financial alignment with COO, CTO, CXO, and CEO
  • Financial systems, tools, and internal financial data governance

3. Governance Principles

The CFO's governance system is built on five core principles.

3.1 Transparency

Financial information must be accurate, timely, unfiltered, and accessible to relevant stakeholders. The CFO ensures the CEO always receives a clear and honest view of the company's financial position.

3.2 Predictability

Forecasts, budgets, and reports must be reliable and consistent. Unexpected deviations from projections are escalated immediately.

3.3 Control & Accountability

The CFO defines approval workflows, access rights, financial controls, and sign-off requirements. The CFO enforces these controls across departments.

3.4 Alignment & Integration

All financial decisions must be aligned with operational realities. The CFO collaborates closely with COO, CTO, CXO, and CEO.

3.5 Scalability

Governance systems must scale seamlessly as Crave'n expands into new markets and higher transaction volumes.

4. Governance Operating Model

The CFO governance model consists of four structural layers.

4.1 Strategic Governance

  • Annual budget and financial plan
  • 3-year strategic financial roadmap
  • Capital allocation strategy
  • Fundraising and investor engagement governance

4.2 Operational Governance

  • Weekly, monthly, and quarterly reporting cadence
  • Cash forecasting and treasury management
  • Expense governance and vendor controls
  • Approval workflows and delegation of authority

4.3 Control Governance

  • Financial controls and segregation of duties
  • Financial data integrity and system access governance
  • Compliance monitoring and regulatory obligations
  • Risk identification, escalation, and mitigation

4.4 Information Governance

  • Ownership of financial systems and financial datasets
  • Reporting standards and metric definitions
  • Data retention and audit readiness
  • Source-of-truth financial data architecture

5. Delegation Of Authority & Escalation

The CFO must operate within the enterprise Delegation of Authority (DOA) framework approved by the CEO. The CFO may further delegate operational-level responsibilities, but accountability remains with the CFO at all times.

5.1 Escalation Triggers

  • Material deviation from projected cash runway
  • Unplanned liabilities, vendor risks, or financial exposures
  • Non-compliance with regulatory or tax filings
  • Suspected fraud, misconduct, or internal control failures
  • Any financial risk that may impact operations or investor confidence

5.2 Escalation Path

  1. CFO → CEO (immediate escalation)
  2. CFO → COO/CTO/CXO (cross-functional impact)
  3. CFO → Board or Advisors (CEO direction required)

6. Governance Cadence

Governance is enforced through a disciplined operating cadence managed by the CFO.

6.1 Weekly

  • Finance snapshot to CEO
  • Cash forecast update
  • Spend review and vendor exposure flags

6.2 Monthly

  • Full financial statement package
  • Variance analysis and performance narrative
  • Compliance and regulatory checkpoint

6.3 Quarterly

  • Strategic business review
  • Pricing, payout, and fee structure review
  • Investor readiness update

7. Governance Outputs

The CFO produces structured governance artifacts that support execution and compliance.

  • Annual financial plan and budget
  • Weekly finance summary
  • Monthly reporting packet
  • Quarterly strategic review packet
  • Compliance and filings schedule
  • Financial risk register
  • Delegation of authority and approval matrix

8. Governance Risk Domains

The CFO monitors risk across the following domains.

  • Liquidity and cash runway
  • Financial accuracy and system integrity
  • Operational cost pressures
  • Pricing and payout economics
  • Vendor and contract exposure
  • Compliance and regulatory risk
  • Investor confidence and reporting consistency

9. Amendment And Review

This Governance Framework will be reviewed annually by the CFO and CEO, or more frequently if required based on business scale or regulatory requirements.
