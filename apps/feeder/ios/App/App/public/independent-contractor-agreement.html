You may request paper copies, but electronic delivery is the default method. You may withdraw this consent by notifying Crave'n, but this may affect your account access. 12. DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND ARBITRATION

12.1 Agreement to Arbitrate. You and Crave'n agree to resolve all disputes through individual arbitration governed by the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. §§ 1-16). This includes disputes about your classification as an independent contractor, Services provided, payments received, Agreement termination, and all aspects of our relationship, whether under federal, state, or local law. Arbitration means a neutral third party (an arbitrator) makes a final, binding decision. There is no judge or jury, and court review is limited.

12.2 Class Actions. Class, collective, and representative actions may affect your rights. You have the right to consult with legal counsel about this arbitration provision.

12.3 Related Third Parties. This arbitration provision applies to disputes with Crave'n's affiliates, subsidiaries, vendors, suppliers, and other related parties. It also applies to claims brought by associations or organizations on your behalf.

12.4 Governing Law for Arbitration. This provision is governed by the Federal Arbitration Act. If the FAA doesn't apply, state arbitration law in the state where you perform most Services (or where you reside if you haven't performed Services) applies.

12.5 Related Disputes. If you have both arbitrable and non-arbitrable disputes, arbitrable disputes proceed first, and non-arbitrable disputes are stayed pending arbitration completion.

12.6 Informal Resolution. Before demanding arbitration, you and Crave'n agree to attempt informal resolution through a good-faith conference (by phone or video) within 60 days of written notice. The initiating party must provide written notice describing the claim. To initiate, email legal@cravenusa.com with your name, phone number, email, and claim description. If informal resolution fails or is declined, you may initiate arbitration 60 days after providing notice. Statutes of limitations are tolled during this process.

12.7 Arbitration Demand. After informal resolution, to initiate arbitration, send a written demand via certified mail or hand delivery within the statute of limitations period. The demand must include: (1) your name and address; (2) legal and factual basis; (3) remedy sought; (4) amount in controversy; and (5) your signature. Send demands to General Counsel, Crave'n Inc, at legal@cravenusa.com.

12.8 Class Action Waiver. You and Crave'n waive the right to participate in class, collective, consolidated, or representative actions. All disputes are resolved individually. If any part of this waiver is unenforceable, the unenforceable part is severed, and the rest remains valid. Class actions must be litigated in court (but stayed pending arbitration of individual claims). For California PAGA claims, individual claims go to arbitration; non-individual claims go to court. Public injunctive relief claims are severed and resolved in court after arbitration.

12.9 Arbitrability Determinations. An arbitrator decides whether this arbitration provision is enforceable, except that a court decides whether the class action waiver is enforceable.

12.10 Arbitration Procedures. Arbitration is administered by JAMS under its Comprehensive Arbitration Rules and Mass Arbitration Procedures. If JAMS is unavailable, the American Arbitration Association administers under its Commercial Arbitration Rules. Key procedures:

  1. One arbitrator with experience in the relevant law;
  2. Location within 45 miles of your residence (if not agreed otherwise);
  3. Fees shared equally, but your share won't exceed court filing fees. Crave'n pays arbitrator fees and room rental if required by law;
  4. Discovery sufficient to prepare claims/defenses, with Apex Doctrine applying to high-level officer depositions;
  5. Arbitrator may award all remedies available in court;
  6. Arbitrator may hear motions to dismiss and summary judgment motions;
  7. Written decision with findings of fact and conclusions of law;
  8. Confidentiality protections for proprietary information;
  9. Either party may seek temporary injunctive relief from a court;
  10. Up to 50 similar claims may be assigned to the same arbitrator, but each resolved individually.

12.11 Government Agency Claims. This provision doesn't prevent you from filing charges with government agencies (EEOC, DOL, NLRB, etc.) or prevent agency investigations. It doesn't prevent exercising rights under the National Labor Relations Act. Crave'n will not retaliate for filing agency claims.

12.12 Opt-Out Right. New Contractors may opt out of arbitration within 30 days of this Agreement's effective date by sending a signed letter by First Class Mail to General Counsel, Crave'n Inc, at legal@cravenusa.com. Email opt-outs are ineffective. The letter must state your intention to opt out and be postmarked within 30 days. Only one Contractor per letter. If you opt out, you won't face adverse action and may pursue legal remedies without arbitration. If you don't opt out within 30 days, you agree to arbitration.
