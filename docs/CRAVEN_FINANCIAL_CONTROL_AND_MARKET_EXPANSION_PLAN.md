# CRAVEN INC. — Financial Control & Market Expansion Plan
**Prepared by:** CEO, CFO, CPO

---

## 1. Executive Objective

Establish a disciplined financial system that:

- Maintains operational stability
- Generates controlled surplus
- Converts surplus into measurable competitive pricing advantage
- Enables structured, repeatable market expansion

This plan eliminates reactive decision-making by replacing it with a controlled, performance-driven operating model.

---

## 2. Financial System Philosophy (Enterprise Controls)

Craven's financial operating model is built on three non-negotiable beliefs:

1. **Baseline first:** The business must remain operationally stable before any expansion or pricing maneuvering occurs.
2. **Surplus governance:** Pricing leverage is funded only by surplus — no arbitrary assumptions, no ungoverned spending.
3. **Structural risk separation:** Execution speed and protection of capital are enforced through account separation, not through human discipline alone.

---

## 3. Core Financial Structure

### 3.1 Banking Architecture

**Execution Layer (Primary — allocation speed + operating engine)**

- Mercury Operating Account
- Mercury Pricing / Growth Account

**Protection Layer (Capital vault — risk isolation)**

- Reserve Account (Chase or existing Bluevine)

### 3.2 Functional Roles

#### Operating Account
- Receives **all Stripe payouts**
- Covers payroll, vendor payments, and core operating expenses
- Anchors monthly baseline burn

#### Pricing / Growth Account
- Holds funds allocated for:
  - Commission reduction strategy
  - Market expansion efforts
- Acts as the competitive lever by funding pricing advantage from governed surplus

#### Reserve Account
- Holds protected capital
- No day-to-day access
- Used only for:
  - Emergency scenarios
  - Planned distributions (dividends / deferred comp)

---

## 4. Baseline & Revenue Logic

### 4.1 Fixed Baseline Burn

- Monthly baseline burn: **~$46,000**

This baseline represents the minimum required to operate without compromising payroll continuity, critical vendors, or execution capacity.

### 4.2 Revenue Flow Logic

1. Stripe payouts -> Mercury Operating
2. First $46K -> retained in Operating (non-negotiable baseline coverage)
3. Any amount above baseline -> classified as Surplus

Surplus is the only source of funds allowed to influence commission economics and expansion investment.

---

## 5. Surplus Allocation Model

Once baseline is covered, surplus is split into three governed buckets.

### 5.1 Allocation Structure

- 20% -> Reserve (Protection)
- 40% -> Retained Business Capital
- 40% -> Pricing / Growth (Market Leverage)

### 5.2 Purpose of Each Allocation

#### Reserve (20%)
- Builds long-term stability
- Supports deferred compensation and future distributions
- Reduces financial risk

#### Retained Business Capital (40%)
- Strengthens internal liquidity
- Supports scaling operations
- Covers unforeseen operational increases

#### Pricing / Growth (40%)
- Funds commission reduction strategy
- Supports targeted expansion into new markets
- Drives competitive positioning and merchant savings

---

## 6. Commission Adjustment System (Surplus-Derived)

### 6.1 Principle

Commission is not manually set.
It is derived from available surplus allocated to pricing.

### 6.2 Calculation Method (Monthly)

1. Identify monthly surplus allocated to pricing
2. Divide by total monthly order volume
3. Translate into per-order leverage value
4. Convert into effective commission % reduction for the next billing cycle

### 6.3 Example (Illustrative)

- Pricing allocation: $20,000
- Orders: 5,000/month
- Per-order leverage: $4

On a $40 order:
- $4 = 10% of order value

Result (controlled example):
- Commission reduced from 15% -> ~12% or lower

### 6.4 Implementation Rules (Non-Negotiable Controls)

- Adjustments occur monthly only
- Changes apply to the next billing cycle
- No mid-cycle or reactive changes
- No discretionary commission changes outside the governance cadence

---

## 7. Operational Phases (Execution Over Time)

### Phase 1 — Stabilization

- Maintain current commission (e.g., 15%)
- Track revenue and burn vs baseline
- No pricing adjustments
- Goal: consistent surplus generation above baseline

### Phase 2 — Controlled Allocation

- Begin surplus allocation (20/40/40)
- Fund Reserve and Pricing / Growth accounts
- Maintain conservative commission reductions based on realized surplus

### Phase 3 — Market Pressure

- Deploy pricing advantage in expansion markets
- Example:
  - Launch new city at 12%
  - Supported by surplus from established markets (e.g., Toledo)

Positioning:
- We are already profitable in existing markets.
- We pass savings directly to merchants.

---

## 8. Expansion Strategy

### 8.1 Market Entry Model
New markets (e.g., Cincinnati) will launch with:

- Lower starting commission (e.g., 12%)
- Backed by surplus from established markets
- Supported by clear merchant-facing savings

### 8.2 Merchant Positioning
- Emphasize real dollar savings vs competitors
- Reinforce platform as:
  - Lower cost
  - More aligned with merchant success

### 8.3 Physical Presence Strategy (Brand Execution)
- Strong in-store branding
- Larger, more visible signage than competitors
- Messaging focused on:
  - Merchant benefit
  - Local alignment

---

## 9. Governance & Controls

### 9.1 Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Baseline ($46K) is always covered first
2. Reserve funds are not used for operations
3. Commission reductions only occur from surplus allocated to pricing
4. Adjustments happen monthly - not reactively
5. Monthly finance review is required for transparency and auditability

### 9.2 Reporting Cadence (Monthly Finance Review)

Monthly review includes:

- Total revenue / settlement inflows
- Baseline coverage status
- Surplus generated
- Allocation breakdown (20/40/40)
- Current commission rate
- Projected next-cycle commission (derived)
- Cash runway implications and reserve movement

---

## 10. Strategic Outcome

This system ensures:

- Financial discipline from day one
- Predictable scaling without cash instability
- Ability to undercut competitors without risk
- A repeatable model for multi-city expansion

---

## 11. Summary

Craven will not compete through uncontrolled spending or arbitrary pricing.

Instead, the company will:

- Operate lean
- Generate surplus
- Convert surplus into pricing advantage
- Scale market by market with control

This creates a system where growth is funded internally and competition is addressed through structured financial leverage.

# CRAVEN INC. — Addendum — Banking Infrastructure Rationale (Mercury + Reserve Strategy)
**Prepared by:** CEO, CFO, CPO

---

## 1. Purpose

This addendum outlines:

- Why Mercury is being used as the primary operating bank
- Why this structure is safe and appropriate
- How risk is mitigated through account separation

This is to ensure full alignment across leadership, specifically finance.

---

## 2. Why Mercury (Primary Operating Bank)

### 2.1 Functional Fit for Our Model
Craven requires:

- Fast internal fund movement (monthly allocations)
- Multiple account segmentation (Operating, Pricing)
- Clean visibility into cash position

Mercury provides:

- Instant transfers between internal accounts
- Ability to create multiple accounts under one entity
- Simple, real-time financial visibility

This directly supports our allocation model:
- Baseline -> Surplus -> Structured distribution

A traditional bank introduces friction here.

### 2.2 Integration with Stripe

- Mercury is designed to work seamlessly with Stripe
- Faster settlement and cleaner reconciliation
- Reduces operational complexity in payout tracking

This is critical since:
- All revenue originates through Stripe
- Timing and clarity of inflows matter for monthly allocation logic

### 2.3 Operational Speed
Our model depends on:

- Monthly allocation cycles
- Rapid reallocation of surplus
- Timely execution of pricing adjustments

Mercury enables:

- Same-day internal transfers
- No manual bottlenecks
- No dependency on external banking delays

This is not a convenience - it is a requirement for execution.

---

## 3. Safety & Risk Considerations

### 3.1 Not a "Standalone Fintech Risk"
Mercury is not holding funds independently.
Funds are held with partner banks:
- FDIC-insured institutions (e.g., Evolve Bank & Trust, Choice Financial)

Coverage:
- Up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank
- With Mercury's sweep structure, coverage can extend beyond a single bank limit

### 3.2 Risk Mitigation Through Structure
We are not relying solely on Mercury.
We are intentionally separating functions:

**Mercury (Execution Layer)**
- Operating funds
- Pricing / growth allocations
- Active cash movement

**Major Bank / Bluevine (Protection Layer)**
- Reserve capital
- No operational exposure
- No daily movement

### 3.3 Why This Matters
Execution funds require speed -> Mercury
Protected funds require stability -> Major bank

This separation ensures:
- We do not expose reserve capital to operational risk
- We maintain liquidity without sacrificing discipline

---

## 4. Why Not Use a Major Bank for Everything

A traditional bank (e.g., Chase) introduces:

- Slower internal transfers
- Limited sub-account flexibility
- Increased friction in monthly allocation execution

This creates:
- Delays in pricing adjustments
- Manual overhead
- Higher likelihood of operational inefficiency

For our model, this is unacceptable.

---

## 5. Control & Discipline

The system is designed to enforce behavior:

- Operating funds remain in Mercury
- Pricing funds remain in Mercury
- Reserve funds are isolated externally

Key rule:
Reserve funds are not used for:
- Operating expenses
- Pricing adjustments

This ensures long-term stability.

---

## 6. Strategic Position

This is not a temporary setup.
This is a deliberate structure:

- Mercury -> engine
- Reserve bank -> vault

This structure allows us to:
- Execute quickly
- Protect capital
- Scale without reworking financial infrastructure

---

## 7. Bottom Line

We are not choosing Mercury over traditional banking.
We are assigning roles:

- Mercury = engine
- Reserve bank = vault

This structure allows us to:
- Execute quickly
- Protect capital
- Scale without reworking financial infrastructure

---

## End of Addendum
